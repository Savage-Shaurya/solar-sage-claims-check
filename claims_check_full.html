1. Efficiency, gain, loss and saving claims

What we checked

Every efficiency / loss / gain / saving number in the report, deck, poster, website and visual report, recomputed from Data_02/solarlink_thapar_daily.csv, lifetime_yearly.csv, site.json, planner.ts and the live /api/campus/plan.

Three findings that matter more than any single number

  1. Cleaning steps do not grow with the gap. From the 113 usable cleaning intervals in site.json: the dust recovered at a cleaning has median 5.5% / mean 6.3% (largest accepted 20%), and its correlation with the days since the previous reset is 0.03. The flat-rate model (0.26 %/day × days) has leave-one-out error 3.89 points; simply predicting a constant step of about 5.5% gives 1.83 points. So "linear soiling at 0.26 %/day for 79 days" is not supported by the plant's own cleaning data. (Caveat: detection only sees steps of 3.5% or more, and rate = step ÷ days builds in a negative rate–days correlation of -0.70.)
  2. Everything in the ₹ and % saving chain is the algebra of that linear assumption. Average loss = r·T/2: 0.258% × 79 d ÷ 2 = 10.2%; 0.258% × 17 d ÷ 2 = 2.2%. The numbers reproduce exactly, but they are model outputs, not measurements. With a typical observed step of about 6%, the real average loss under current practice is likely lower than 10.2% and the gain from a 17-day routine smaller than 8 points. Treat them as an upper-end modelled estimate until a cleaning log exists.
  3. Chapter 7C says the opposite of 5.7.9. 7C: "no monetary saving or payback period is claimed". Section 5.7.9, deck slides 15, 23, 25, the poster and the visual report all quote ₹3.7 lakh/yr. One of them must change.

4. Performance Ratio, explained again

Standard definition

IEC 61724-1 ("Photovoltaic system performance – Monitoring", IEC webstore): PR = Yf ÷ Yr.

  • Final yield Yf = energy delivered E (kWh) ÷ installed DC capacity P0 (kWp). Units kWh/kWp, i.e. "hours of full output".
  • Reference yield Yr = plane-of-array irradiation H (kWh/m²) ÷ 1 kW/m². It is the number of "peak sun hours" the panel plane received.
  • So PR is the share of the sunlight-implied energy that actually reached the meter. Losses it contains: temperature, soiling, wiring, mismatch, inverter, clipping, downtime, shading. A temperature-corrected PR (also in the standard) divides out the temperature loss.

What the Solar Link portal computes (checked on our files)

  • Daily: on 320 days with all three values, the portal's PR equals Yield_kWh_per_kWp ÷ POA Energy to within 0.5 points on 86% of days. Larger gaps are in Oct–Nov 2025 (partial logging) and the 2026 pyranometer-fault days.
  • Yearly: the 2026 row says PR 88.1; 1,332.1 kWh/kWp ÷ 1,512.1 kWh/m² (the GHI energy) = 88.10%. The portal is configured "GTI(POA)/GHI = GHI". The single pyranometer is on G Block only.
  • The live snapshot also shows a "temp-corrected PR 86.9%" next to PR 86.8%.

Worked example with real numbers: 15 Oct 2026

Energy at meters 10,838.5 kWh; Yf = 3.67 kWh/kWp (10,838.5 ÷ 2,952.24); POA energy = 4.21 kWh/m² so Yr = 4.21 h. PR = 3.67 ÷ 4.21 = 87.2%; portal shows 87.24%.
Our performance index that day (plantSeries): PI = 0.902.

Typical good values

Roughly 75–85% is the usual range quoted for working grid-tied plants, newer well-run plants reach 80–90% (general industry knowledge, not from the project files). Our plant's monthly portal PR runs 81% (May) to 95% (Nov); the 95% is inflated by the pyranometer fault.
